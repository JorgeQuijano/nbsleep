Next-sleep helper

A “wake window” is how long your baby can comfortably be awake before the next sleep. It grows week by week — this uses your baby’s current age.

Trust sleepy cues over the clock: the first window of the morning is shortest, the one before bed is the longest.

Feeding & growth

Feed on demand — hunger cues (rooting, hand to mouth, lip smacking) come before crying, which is a late sign. In the early weeks, don’t let a newborn go longer than ~4 h between feeds, day or night, unless your pediatrician has ok’d it.

Signs baby is getting enough

  • Wet diapers: day 1: 1–2 → by day 5–7: 6+ per day, pale yellow.
  • Poops: black/tarry → green → by day 5: yellow & seedy; often 3–8/day until ~6 weeks, then fewer.
  • Weight: back to birth weight by day 10–14, gaining steadily after.
  • Content after feeds; you can see/hear swallowing.

Call your pediatrician if…

  • Fewer than 6 wet or fewer than 3 dirty diapers a day by day 5.
  • Baby is still losing weight after day 5.
  • Fewer than 8 feeds in 24 h most days, or very hard to wake for feeds.
  • Fever, jaundice getting deeper, or baby seems unusually limp/floppy.

Safe sleep (AAP)

  • Back for every sleep — naps and nights. If baby rolls both ways on their own, let them choose their position.
  • Firm, flat surface — CPSC-approved crib / bassinet / play yard, tight fitted sheet only. No inclined surfaces (over ~10° is unsafe).
  • Room-share, don’t bed-share — same room as you (not the same bed) for at least the first 6 months; it can cut SIDS risk by up to 50%. Bed-sharing risk is 5–10× higher under 4 months.
  • Keep the sleep space bare — no pillows, blankets, bumpers, toys, positioners; never weighted blankets or weighted swaddles.
  • Don’t overheat — one layer more than you’re wearing; no hats indoors; check for sweating or a hot chest.
  • Pacifier at nap & bedtime — once breastfeeding is established, it’s linked to lower SIDS risk.
  • Found asleep elsewhere? — car seat, stroller, swing, sling: move baby to a firm flat surface as soon as you can.
  • Smoke-free air around baby, always.

Tummy time (awake & supervised)

Start the day you get home: 2–3 short sessions of 3–5 minutes. Work up to 15–30 minutes a day by 7 weeks. It builds neck/shoulder strength and prevents flat spots — just never for sleep, and never unattended.

Sleepy cues & settling

Getting sleepy

  • Yawning, jerky movements
  • Staring into space / zoning out
  • Looking away, losing interest
  • Fussing — this is the late cue in a newborn; start the wind-down at the first yawn

Already overtired

  • Hard to settle, crying ramps fast
  • Naps under 30 minutes
  • Extra night wakings
  • Fix: shorten wake windows by 10–15 minutes for a few days

Tips that actually help

  • Day/night steering: days bright, chatty and interactive; nights dark, quiet and boring (no play). Cap any single daytime nap at 2–3 h in the first weeks.
  • Swaddle + white noise + dark room for newborns (stop swaddling at first signs of rolling).
  • Eat → wake → sleep rhythm: with newborns, feed right after wake so sleep isn’t tied to feeding.
  • Wakings are normal: many babies 6–12 months old still wake 1–3× a night, and night feeds can stay normal through the first year — especially if breastfeeding.

Growth spurts & regressions

Sleep doesn’t break after the newborn days — it reorganizes. These are the classic windows parents hit:

  1. ~2–3 weeks — growth spurt: hungrier, fussier for a day or two.
  2. ~6 weeks — growth spurt; fussiness often peaks; evening cluster feeds.
  3. ~8 weeks — growth spurt; the first long night stretch often appears here.
  4. ~3–4 months — the “4-month regression”: sleep cycles mature to adult-style 45–60 min; more wakings and short naps for 2–6 weeks. It’s development, not damage.
  5. ~8–10 months — separation anxiety + crawling/standing: 2–4 weeks of disruptions, common.
  6. ~12 months — walking and a language burst: 2–3 weeks.

Regressions pass. Keep responses boring and consistent, protect the routine, and check in with your pediatrician if you’re worried.
